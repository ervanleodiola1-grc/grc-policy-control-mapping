# Incident Response Policy

**Company:** Example Co. (fictional)
**Version:** 1.0
**Owner:** [Your name], IT Security
**Last reviewed:** [Date]

## 1. Purpose

This policy describes how Example Co. detects, responds to, and recovers from security incidents so that damage is limited and the company can learn from each event.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and interns, and to all company systems and data.

## 3. Definitions

3.1 A **security event** is any observable occurrence that might affect security, such as a failed login or a suspicious email.

3.2 A **security incident** is an event that actually or potentially harms the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of company systems or data.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

4.1 **All staff** must report suspected incidents immediately.

4.2 **The Incident Response Lead** (IT Security) coordinates the response and decides the severity level.

4.3 **Management** is informed of high and critical incidents and approves major decisions, such as taking a system offline.

4.4 **HR and Legal** are involved when an incident affects employees, customer data, or legal obligations.

## 5. Detection and Reporting

5.1 Anyone who notices a possible incident must report it to IT Security by email or phone within 1 hour.

5.2 Security tools (such as antivirus and log monitoring) generate alerts, which IT Security reviews daily.

5.3 Every reported incident is recorded in an incident log with the date, time, reporter, and a short description.

## 6. Severity Levels

| Level | Description | Example | Target response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | Minor issue, no data exposed | One blocked phishing email | 2 business days |
| Medium | Limited impact on one user or system | One compromised user account | 1 business day |
| High | Significant impact or possible data exposure | Malware on several devices | 4 hours |
| Critical | Major outage or confirmed data breach | Ransomware, customer data stolen | 1 hour |

## 7. Response Steps

7.1 **Contain:** Limit the damage, for example by disconnecting affected devices or disabling compromised accounts.

7.2 **Investigate:** Work out what happened, what was affected, and how it started. Preserve evidence such as logs.

7.3 **Eradicate:** Remove the cause, such as deleting malware or fixing the vulnerability.

7.4 **Recover:** Restore systems and data from clean backups and confirm they work normally before returning to use.

## 8. Communication

8.1 Internal updates go to management at a frequency set by the severity level.

8.2 External communication (customers, regulators, media) is handled only by designated staff, after approval from management and Legal.

## 9. Post-Incident Review

9.1 After every medium, high, or critical incident, a review meeting is held within 10 business days.

9.2 The review documents what happened, what worked, what did not, and what will change.

9.3 Agreed improvements are assigned an owner and a due date.

## 10. Testing and Training

10.1 Staff receive incident reporting training when they join and once a year after that.

10.2 The response process is tested once a year with a tabletop exercise (a talk-through of a made-up incident).

## 11. Enforcement

Failing to report a known incident may lead to disciplinary action.

## 12. Review

This policy is reviewed once a year, or sooner after a major incident.
